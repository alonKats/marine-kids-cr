02Overview

Program overview

A marine biology program design, aligned with IB PYP and MEP standards and built around the internationally recognized Ocean Literacy framework (NOAA / NMEA).

The program combines hands-on in-school learning and excursions, using our coastline as a living classroom and connecting students directly with marine ecosystems through fieldwork and scientific inquiry.

06Scope & sequence

Proposed progression by grade

An example scaffolded framework — revised and finalized together with Journey to fit your standards and curriculum.
K–2
"The ocean is alive, and I've met it."

Sensory exploration and storytelling. Introduction to marine life and habitats.

Grades 3–5
"The ocean is a system, and I can describe it."

Understanding ecosystems and human impact. Introduction to data collection and scientific vocabulary.

Grades 6–8
"The ocean has problems, and I can investigate them."

Ecosystem dynamics and environmental challenges. Water-quality testing and citizen science.

Grades 9–12
"The ocean has answers, and I can find them."

Research-based learning and field investigations. Marine policy, conservation, and data analysis.

07In practice

Instruction methods

Each lesson is 90 minutes. Draws on data sets from NOAA, Monterey Bay Aquarium, Woods Hole, and other leaders in the field.

Hands-on activities

Build a rocky shore model. Scavenger hunts. Specimen observation. Plankton towing.

Field excursions

Tide pools at Avellanas / Negra. Sea turtle nesting. Mangrove and estuary exploration. Marine agriculture in the Gulf of Nicoya.

Data collection and logs

Water clarity. Beach debris counts. Plankton sampling. Microplastics analysis. Ecological survey and citizen science.

08The ask

Two ways in

Option 1 · Recommended

A 1-year program, beginning in December.

Based on 9–10 months of school.

  • Monthly visits with a marine biologist
  • 1 field excursion per semester (3 total)
  • 9 × 90-minute hands-on, PBL lessons
  • Worksheets, field guides, and classroom materials included
  • Differentiation strategies to support diverse learners
  • 2–3 professional development sessions (Train-the-Trainer)
Option 2 · Lighter touch

One project over a few months.

  • One ocean literacy project per grade level
  • 1 field excursion
  • Number of lessons TBD with the program
  • Project-based assessments
  • Worksheets, field guides, and classroom materials included
  • Differentiation strategies to support diverse learners
  • 2 teacher training sessions
