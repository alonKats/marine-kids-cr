## Slide 2 — Why this fits Journey (the 3 reasons)

**This program isn't a bolt-on. It's three things Journey already wants, in one frame.**

- **Standards-aligned, inside the existing curriculum.** Built on Ocean Literacy (NOAA/NMEA), mapped to IB PYP "Sharing the Planet" and MEP biodiversity standards. We integrate — we don't add a new slot. *You teach the science you're already mandated to teach, just done well.*
- **A real draw for parents.** Families in Guanacaste live near the ocean but rarely engage with it scientifically. A school-embedded Ocean Literacy program is a visible differentiator for admissions — and a credential pathway (FEE Green Flag, MSA/Cognia portfolio).
- **Career exposure starting middle school.** Most students don't know marine careers exist — boat captain, researcher, conservation officer, marine policy. The program lets them picture themselves in those futures, not just learn about them.

> **Say this:** *"There are three reasons I think this fits Journey specifically — and any one of them on its own would be reason enough."*

## Slide 3 — What is Ocean Literacy?

**An internationally recognized framework, not my invention.**

- Developed by NOAA / NMEA, used in 100+ countries.
- Built around **7 core principles**, scaffolded across K–12 with 45 supporting concepts.
- Maps directly onto **IB PYP "Sharing the Planet"** and **MEP Ciencias Naturales — biodiversidad marina.**

**The 7 principles** (one-line read):
1. Earth has one big ocean with many features
2. The ocean shapes the features of Earth
3. The ocean drives weather and climate
4. The ocean made Earth habitable
5. The ocean supports diversity of life
6. The ocean and humans are interconnected
7. The ocean is largely unexplored

> **Say this:** *"MEP mandates that biodiversidad marina be taught — but most schools fulfill it with terrestrial content because they don't have a marine biologist. I fill that gap."*

## Slide 4 — How it scales by grade

**Same framework, four cognitive tiers — each matches stage to a different *kind* of marine work.**

| Grade | Frame | What students do |
|---|---|---|
| **K–2** (PYP early) | *The ocean is alive, and I've met it.* | Storytelling, touch tanks, habitat models, tide-pool encounters |
| **3–5** (PYP late) | *The ocean is a system, and I can describe it.* | Ecosystems, beach-debris counts, water-clarity logs, vocabulary build |
| **6–8** (MYP) | *The ocean has problems, and I can investigate them.* | Water-quality testing, citizen science, ecosystem dynamics |
| **9–12** (DP / CAS) | *The ocean has answers, and I can find them.* | Research-question fieldwork, marine policy, NOAA / PRETOMA datasets |

> **Say this:** *"The pilot enters at one tier — wherever you have the strongest energy. The framework is built so it scales to the rest in Year 2."*

## Slide 5 — What students actually do

**Lessons are 90 minutes. Hands-on first, theory second.**

- **Hands-on activities.** Build a rocky-shore model. Observe specimens. Tow plankton.
- **Field excursions.** Tide pools at Avellanas/Negra. Sea turtle nesting. Mangrove/estuary. Marine agriculture in the Gulf of Nicoya.
- **Real data collection.** Water clarity. Beach debris. Plankton sampling. Microplastics. Citizen science contributions to actual datasets.
- **The local advantage.** Olive ridleys nest at Ostional Aug–Nov (45 min away). Leatherbacks at Las Baulas Oct–Mar (15 min). **Both during the school year.** A Grade 5 unit built around a real nest count is real science, not a simulation.

> **Say this:** *"No PowerPoint beats *'the turtles are nesting 20 minutes from here, and your Grade 7 class can document it.'*"*

## Slide 6 — Two ways in

**Option 1 — Full-year pilot (recommended)**
- Begins December
- Monthly visits with marine biologist
- 9 × 90-min hands-on lessons
- 3 field excursions (one per term)
- 2–3 teacher PD sessions (train-the-trainer)
- Worksheets, field guides, differentiation materials
- Designed to renew Year 2 with teachers running ~40% independently

**Option 2 — Single project, a few months**
- One ocean literacy project per grade level
- 1 field excursion
- Lesson count adapted to scope
- Project-based assessments
- 2 teacher training sessions

> **Say this:** *"Both options come with a written curriculum, materials, and a measured learning outcome. Option 1 is a year; Option 2 is a semester or trimester. We can also do Option 2 first as a pilot, and graduate to Option 1 in Year 2 once you've seen the work."*
